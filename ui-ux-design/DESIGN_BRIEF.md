# Kuvo UI/UX Design Brief

**Status:** Working direction
**Scope:** Mobile product identity and high-fidelity prototype
**Audience:** Young Nigerians, beginning with campus communities

## Design problem

Kuvo needs to look distinct from generic messaging clones while staying fast to understand on a small screen and comfortable for frequent conversations.

The imported prototype already establishes a useful product structure: onboarding, a four-tab shell, chat lists, message views, contacts, status, profile, settings, and a new-chat entry point. This pass adds stronger brand character without sacrificing that structure.

## Hard constraints

- Messaging content remains the highest-priority information.
- Touch targets must stay comfortable on low-end Android devices.
- Body text must remain highly readable at mobile sizes.
- Navigation patterns should feel familiar.
- Color and decoration must not reduce contrast or obscure status.
- The identity must work for both campus users and the future business surface.

## Soft constraints

- The existing soft-depth treatment can become flatter and more graphic.
- Rounded cards can keep their friendly shape while gaining heavier outlines.
- The current green, purple, orange, and light background palette can remain, but screens should use fewer accents at once.
- Typography can become more expressive in onboarding, empty states, and promotional moments than in active chat.

## Chosen design axes

### 1. Aesthetic: controlled neo-brutalism

Use flat color, black outlines, and offset shadows for primary actions, cards, and identity moments. Avoid applying the treatment to every message bubble or list row.

### 2. Structural: calm core, expressive edges

Keep the bottom navigation, chat hierarchy, and familiar back behavior from the prototype. Concentrate the strongest visual statements in onboarding, new-chat creation, empty states, and the profile header.

### 3. Conceptual: the pulse as a social signal

Treat the Kuvo mark as a compact pulse or peak inside a speech bubble. It should suggest an active network without becoming a literal waveform or a complicated letterform.

## Evaluation questions

- Can a first-time user identify how to start a chat in under five seconds?
- Does the visual system remain calm when a screen contains many conversations?
- Is the logo recognizable at 24px and at app-icon size?
- Does the style feel Nigerian and energetic without relying on stereotypes?
- Do the thick borders and offset shadows support hierarchy instead of becoming decoration?
